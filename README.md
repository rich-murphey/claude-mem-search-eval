# claude-mem-search-eval

How accurately does [claude-mem](https://github.com/thedotmack/claude-mem)'s
search by meaning find the right memory? This repo measures precision, recall
and F1 of that search on public data, with the embeddings precomputed so a
result can be checked in minutes rather than recomputed in hours.

## Results

Three datasets, each answering a different question:

- **LongMemEval** answers whether a model change costs English memory search
  accuracy: chat-memory questions whose answers are spread over sessions of
  many turns, the closest public data to claude-mem's own observations.
- **memsearch** answers "does it help in our domain": Claude Code developer
  memory in Chinese (and machine-translated English), with queries and labels
  generated by an LLM. Its recall-based metrics compare models; its P@5 and
  F1@5 are capped by single-answer queries and are not absolute measures.
- **MIRACL** answers "how accurate is it, measured properly": human-judged
  relevance in English, Chinese, Korean and Japanese, with explicit
  non-relevant judgments and two to three relevant passages per query, so
  P@5, R@5 and F1@5 mean something, and J@5 bounds the unjudged share of each
  top 5.

Every results file in `results/` opens with the same statement for its
dataset. All of them are best treated as relative accuracy between models; see
"Caveat on absolute accuracy".

What they show:

- **multilingual-e5-small is the most accurate of the four on every dataset
  but the Chinese ones, English included.** On LongMemEval it is slightly ahead of all-MiniLM-L6-v2
  (F1@5 +0.010, paired 95% interval 0.001–0.019); on MIRACL en, +0.087.
- **all-MiniLM-L6-v2 finds almost nothing in Chinese, Korean or Japanese**
  (MIRACL F1@5 0.001–0.011), and multilingual-MiniLM-L12 recovers less than
  half of what e5-small does there. In English, L12 ties L6.
- **granite-embedding-97m-multilingual-r2 is the most accurate on Chinese and
  behind e5-small elsewhere.** Paired against e5-small, vector-only F1@5:
  +0.052 (0.032–0.070) on MIRACL zh and +0.031 (0.024–0.037) on memsearch zh;
  −0.012 to −0.030 on memsearch en, LongMemEval and MIRACL en and ja; MIRACL
  ko −0.019 (−0.041–0.004). Cut at 512 tokens it scores exactly as at its
  trained 8,192 on five datasets; on MIRACL ko and ja the paired F1@5
  difference is −0.003 (−0.007–0.000) and +0.000, so the cut costs nothing
  measurable.
- **claude-mem's server search ranks exactly as its vectors do.** Vector-only
  search through `PostgresObservationRepository.search` reproduces the exact
  results to the third decimal (HNSW is approximate; a few cells differ by
  0.001).
- **Hybrid search (keyword and vector by reciprocal rank fusion) helps a weak
  vector leg and can hurt a strong one.** It lifts MiniLM-L6 on memsearch and
  MIRACL en, adds nothing to e5-small on LongMemEval, and costs e5-small
  0.07 F1@5 on MIRACL ko, where the keyword leg is poor.
- **Keyword search does not work for CJK questions in either path.** The
  worker answers a query in an unsegmented script by requiring each run of
  it as a literal substring; a question is a few long runs no document
  contains, so every Chinese, Japanese and Korean query returned nothing
  (0 of 200 sampled queries per dataset have a document containing all their
  runs). The server's English text search does no better on MIRACL zh and ja.
- **One store per user is much harder than one haystack per question.** With
  all 19,195 LongMemEval sessions in one project, F1@5 falls from about 0.49
  to 0.13–0.18; e5-small stays ahead, and granite falls to MiniLM-L6's level
  (0.127 vector only, against 0.163 for e5-small).

## Data

### LongMemEval

[LongMemEval](https://github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval), cleaned
(`xiaowu0162/longmemeval-cleaned`, MIT, Wu et al.), the `_s` file: 500
questions, each over its own haystack of about 47 chat sessions, each labelled
with the sessions that hold its answer. The file is downloaded from Hugging
Face at a pinned revision and checked against its sha256
(`src/longmemeval.ts`); it is not redistributed here, nor is any other
dataset.

- 470 questions have answers; the 30 abstention questions (`*_abs`) have no
  relevant session and are left out.
- 300 of the 470 have two to six relevant sessions, so precision and F1 are
  not just recall divided by k.
- 19,195 distinct sessions, 199,509 turns, 198 million characters.
- 12 answerable questions list one session (never an answer session) twice
  in their haystack; each session is counted once.

#### Label errors

Checked in this repo's preparation (2026-10-18), over the 470 answerable
questions. The text is native English, not translated, but much of it is
machine-generated: UltraChat dialogues, ShareGPT chats and LLM-simulated
evidence sessions.

**False positives in the labels** (labelled as holding the answer, but do
not): 45 of 890 answer sessions (5.1%) have no turn marked `has_answer`. They
fall in 32 questions (22 temporal-reasoning, 8 multi-session, 2
knowledge-update); no question has only such labels, and in an affected
question they average 46% of its labels. Of three sampled, none holds the
answer: two are distractors on a nearby date (a different event "today", for a
question about "a month ago"), which help a reasoner rule out the wrong event
but do not contain the answer, and one (for a magazine-subscription count)
never mentions magazines or subscriptions. At most 45 sessions, assuming
sessions with marked turns are right; those were not checked.

**False negatives in the labels** (hold the answer, but are not labelled): a
literal search for each short answer string in unlabelled sessions hit 64 of
298 questions; a sample of 12 hits were all coincidences ("two", "Tom", "$300"
in unrelated text), none an unlabelled answer. The cleaned release exists to
remove sessions that interfered with answers, but paraphrased answers cannot
be found by literal search, so how many remain is unknown.

No session outside `answer_session_ids` has a turn marked `has_answer`.

#### What label errors do to the results

| Label error | Search result | Really | Scored as | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| False positive | Retrieved | False positive | True positive | Precision too high |
| False positive | Not retrieved | True negative | False negative | Recall too low |
| False negative | Retrieved | True positive | False positive | Precision too low |
| False negative | Not retrieved | False negative | True negative (ignored) | Recall too high |

The false positives' effect is bounded: 45 sessions can fill at most 45 of the
2,350 top-5 slots, so P@5 is overstated by at most 1.9 points, and recall is
understated by at most 3.1 points if none of them is retrieved. Both
concentrate in temporal reasoning. The false negatives' effect has no bound
without judging (plan step 5).

#### Caveat on absolute accuracy

Precision and recall here are measured against LongMemEval's labels, which
contain errors in both directions. About 5% of labelled answer sessions (45 of
890, mostly temporal-reasoning distractors) do not hold the answer; these can
raise P@5 by at most 1.9 points and lower recall by at most 3.1. Sessions that
hold an answer without being labelled could not be counted: a literal search
found none, but paraphrased ones cannot be ruled out, and each one retrieved
lowers measured precision while each one missed raises measured recall. The two
errors partly offset, so neither metric is a strict bound. Differences between
models on the same questions are affected far less, since both are scored
against the same labels; per-type results for temporal reasoning are the least
reliable.

### memsearch

[memsearch-memory-retrieval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zc277584121/memsearch-memory-retrieval)
(MIT, revision `dbbdc710`): 955 chunks of developer memory written by
memsearch's Claude Code plugin across 12 projects, in Chinese (the original)
and English (translated by gpt-4o-mini), with 2,172 queries per language.
gpt-4o-mini wrote each query from a chunk and labelled that chunk as its
answer: 1,881 queries have one relevant chunk and 291 multi-hop queries have
two to five. Each chunk is one claude-mem observation in a project named for
its memsearch project, and each query searches its own project.

Labels are probably right where they exist (the answer is the chunk the query
was written from) and incomplete (another chunk that answers is unlabelled), so
recall and rank metrics compare models well, and precision and F1 are capped
and understated.

### MIRACL

[MIRACL](https://project-miracl.github.io/) dev queries in English, Chinese,
Korean and Japanese, with relevance judged by native speakers, through MTEB's
pooled corpus (`mteb/MIRACLRetrievalHardNegatives`, CC-BY-SA-4.0, revision
`332a9acb`): each language's passages are those several retrievers ranked
highly for its queries, plus every judged passage, rather than all of
Wikipedia. Each passage (title and text) is one claude-mem observation, and
each language is one project.

| Language | Queries | Passages | Judged per query | Relevant per query |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| en | 799 | 178,768 | 10.5 | 2.9 |
| zh | 393 | 81,309 | 10.0 | 2.5 |
| ko | 213 | 43,293 | 14.4 | 2.6 |
| ja | 860 | 185,319 | 9.7 | 2.1 |

Judgments cover about ten passages per query, so a model's top 5 can hold
passages nobody judged; they count as not relevant. J@5 reports the judged
share of each top 5, and true P@5 lies between P@5 and P@5 + (1 − J@5). On a
pooled corpus this measures ranking among candidates several retrievers
already found, which is a relative measure of models, but on human labels.

## Models

| Key | Model | Tokens | Prefixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `minilm-l6` | `Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (claude-mem's and Chroma's default) | 256 | none |
| `multilingual-minilm-l12` | `Xenova/paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2` (the #3422 model) | 128 | none |
| `multilingual-e5-small` | `Xenova/multilingual-e5-small` | 512 | `query: ` / `passage: ` |
| `granite-97m-r2-512` | `onnx-community/granite-embedding-97m-multilingual-r2-ONNX` (IBM, added 2026-10-19), cut at 512 tokens as claude-mem's models table carries it | 512 | none |
| `granite-97m-r2` | the same model at its trained length | 8192 | none |

All four produce 384-dimensional vectors, so any of them fits claude-mem's
existing `vector(384)` column.

Each document part (a LongMemEval turn, a memsearch chunk, a MIRACL passage)
is one vector, as claude-mem embeds each part of an observation:
transformers.js 4.3.1 with onnxruntime-node 1.21.0, fp32, mean pooling
(granite: the `[CLS]` token, as its sentence-transformers config pools), L2
normalised, truncated at the model's trained length with the closing
`[SEP]`/`</s>` restored (`src/encoder.ts` says why), stored as fp16.

## Metrics

A document scores as its best part, as claude-mem scores an observation by
its nearest part. k = 5 is claude-mem's per-prompt injection limit, and every
search returns ten documents.

| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P@5 | Share of the top 5 documents that are relevant |
| R@5 | Share of relevant documents in the top 5 |
| F1@5 | Harmonic mean of P@5 and R@5 |
| R@10 | Share of relevant documents in the top 10 |
| R-prec | Precision at k = the number of relevant documents; fair to queries with one relevant document, where P@5 cannot exceed 0.2 |
| MRR@10 | 1 / rank of the first relevant document, 0 if none is in the top 10 |
| nDCG@10 | Rank-discounted gain over the top 10 |
| J@5 | MIRACL only: share of the top 5 that was judged at all |

Intervals are 95%, from 2,000 bootstrap resamples over queries with a fixed
seed. A paired difference is the mean of per-query differences between two
runs on the same queries; it cancels what the queries share and is the
reliable comparison between models.

## Reproducing

Datasets are `longmemeval-s`, `memsearch-zh`, `memsearch-en`, `miracl-en`,
`miracl-zh`, `miracl-ko` and `miracl-ja`.

```sh
npm install
# Tier 0, about a minute: download the release vectors into out/<dataset>/, then
scripts/fetch.sh v2 longmemeval-s                              # checks SHA256SUMS
node src/verify.ts --dataset longmemeval-s --model minilm-l6   # re-embeds 200 rows; fails below cosine 0.9999
node src/eval.ts --dataset longmemeval-s                       # every metric, exactly, from the vectors
# Tier 1: a 100-question LongMemEval sample
node src/eval.ts --dataset longmemeval-s --manifest manifests/lme-s-100-v1.txt
# Tier 2: re-embed a dataset (minutes to hours per model on a CPU; see Timing)
scripts/embed-model.sh longmemeval-s minilm-l6 4 8
# Through claude-mem itself, from a claude-mem checkout with this work, in a
# throwaway pgvector Postgres (and, for the worker, chroma-mcp through uvx):
bun src/server-search.ts --claude-mem <checkout> --db <postgres url> --dataset longmemeval-s
bun src/worker-search.ts --claude-mem <checkout> --dataset longmemeval-s
# Through chroma-mem: export rows and scopes, search there, score its rankings
node src/export-rows.ts --dataset longmemeval-s
node src/eval.ts --dataset longmemeval-s --models minilm-l6 --rankings chroma-mem=<rankings.tsv>
```

`manifests/lme-s-100-v1.txt` and `-v2.txt` are stratified 100-question
samples (`src/manifest.ts`): proportional by question type, chosen by
sha256(seed + question_id), so any language reproduces them from the seed.

### Release files

Vectors are release assets, not git objects. Per dataset and model,
`<dataset>.<model>.rows.f16.bin` holds one little-endian fp16 row of 384
values per line of `<dataset>.rows.tsv`, and `<dataset>.<model>.queries.f16.bin`
one row per line of `<dataset>.queries.tsv`. `rows.tsv` carries each row's
document id, part number and the first 16 hex digits of its text's sha256,
which `verify.ts` checks against the dataset. `SHA256SUMS` covers every file.

## Timing

So others can estimate what checking these results costs. Everything ran on
one AMD EPYC 7351P (16 cores, 32 threads, 125 GB RAM), CPU only, on
2026-10-18, sharing the host with other work (load average 24–37 during the
MIRACL runs), so times are upper bounds.

Embedding, `scripts/embed-model.sh <dataset> <model> 4 8` (4 shards of 8
threads; queries included), wall time in seconds:

| Dataset | Rows | MiniLM-L6 | MiniLM-L12 | e5-small | granite (512) | granite (8192) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LongMemEval | 199,509 | 1,593 | 1,996 | 5,852 | 8,385 | 12,813 |
| memsearch zh | 955 | 30 | 42 | 62 | 99 | 81 |
| memsearch en | 955 | 23 | 38 | 60 | 86 | 76 |
| MIRACL en | 178,768 | 1,209 | 1,711 | 3,056 | 4,670 | 4,710 |
| MIRACL zh | 81,309 | 539 | 688 | 1,100 | 2,192 | 2,544 |
| MIRACL ko | 43,293 | 532 | 447 | 810 | 1,489 | 1,744 |
| MIRACL ja | 185,319 | 1,562 | 1,815 | 3,335 | 6,591 | 6,865 |

The first 21 took 7.4 hours; e5-small, with a 512-token limit, costs two to four
times MiniLM-L6 per row on long text. granite ran on 2026-10-19, under the
same load: 6.5 hours at 512 tokens, 1.6 times e5-small, and 8.0 hours at
8,192, where one 8,192-token text takes about 10 s and 8 GB (attention grows
with the square of its length; `src/encoder.ts` batches to a fixed budget,
and `embed-model.sh` runs in a 64 GB systemd scope). Re-embedding only
memsearch takes minutes.

Checking the published vectors instead:

- `verify.ts`, 200 rows: 8–46 s per dataset and model (all 35 matched at
  cosine 1.0000000).
- `eval.ts`, all five: about 10 s for LongMemEval and memsearch; 98 s (ko),
  344 s (zh), and 28–30 minutes each for MIRACL en and ja, whose queries
  each scan the whole language's corpus (three models: 74 s, 279 s and about
  23 minutes).
- `server-search.ts`, all three models: loading and indexing the vectors in
  Postgres dominates, about 3.5 minutes per model for LongMemEval and up to
  10 minutes per model for MIRACL en and ja; searches take seconds to a
  minute. 12 minutes for LongMemEval scoped, 32–40 minutes for each large
  MIRACL language, 2–4 minutes for memsearch. granite's load and index took
  14–18 minutes per large dataset, with embedding running alongside.
- `worker-search.ts`: keyword search alone takes 1–8 minutes; with Chroma,
  chroma-mcp embeds every document part itself, about 6–8 minutes for
  memsearch, 8 minutes for the 14 LongMemEval questions with five or more
  relevant sessions and 53 minutes for the 100-question sample. That is why
  worker search by meaning was not run on the full LongMemEval or MIRACL.

Release v1 holds the first three models, about 1.6 GB (440 MB LongMemEval,
395 and 410 MB MIRACL en and ja, 180 MB zh, 96 MB ko, 7 MB per memsearch
language). Release v2 adds both granite cuts, 535 MB each, for 2.7 GB; its
first three models' files are identical to v1's.

## Plan

1. **Vector-only accuracy**, exact, from the precomputed vectors: done for
   all three datasets and all models.
2. **Through claude-mem's server search**: done; each dataset in a throwaway
   pgvector Postgres, searched three ways (keyword only, vector only, and the
   shipped hybrid with reciprocal rank fusion), reusing these vectors.
3. **Through claude-mem's worker search**: done; SQLite FTS5 keyword search
   on every dataset, and Chroma search, with chroma-mcp embedding the
   documents itself, on memsearch and LongMemEval samples.
4. **One store per user**: done for LongMemEval and memsearch; every document
   in one project rather than one haystack per question.
5. **Judged precision**: an LLM pass with a hand-checked sample over top-5
   results that are not labelled relevant, to bound the unlabelled answers.
